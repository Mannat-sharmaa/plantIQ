# Guava (Psidium guajava L.) Horticultural & Health Guide

## Botanical Profile and Foliar Morphology
Guava (*Psidium guajava*), belonging to the family Myrtaceae, is a vital tropical and subtropical fruit crop. Healthy guava trees exhibit opposite, simple, oblong to elliptic leaves with prominent pinnate venation. Young foliage is light green and pubescent, maturing into coriaceous (leathery) dark green leaves. Developing young fruits display persistent calyx lobes at the apex.

## Foliar Health and Diagnostic Baseline
A healthy guava tree exhibits:
1. Vibrant deep green foliage without interveinal chlorosis, marginal scorch, or bronzing.
2. Smooth, unblemished fruit surface without sunken circular necrotic lesions.
3. Sturdy branch architecture capable of bearing developing fruit load.

## Common Phytopathological Risks in Guava
1. **Guava Wilt (*Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. psidii*)**: Manifests as yellowing and sudden wilting of leaves on one or more branches. Avoid waterlogging and ensure well-aerated soil.
2. **Anthracnose (*Colletotrichum gloeosporioides*)**: Causes dark brown circular sunken necrotic spots on tender twigs and developing fruits.
3. **Algal Leaf Spot (*Cephaleuros virescens*)**: Rusty reddish-orange velvety patches on upper leaf surfaces during monsoon periods.
4. **Fruit Fly (*Bactrocera correcta / dorsalis*)**: Larvae burrow into maturing fruit. Prevent with methyl eugenol pheromone traps and fruit bagging.

## Integrated Agronomic Management & Cultivation Practices
1. **Canopy Training & Pruning**: Light annual pruning after harvest stimulates new vegetative flushes which bear future blossoms. Maintain open center for sunlight penetration.
2. **Irrigation Schedule**: Deep watering during fruit development intervals (every 7-10 days in dry seasons). Avoid standing water around the root flare.
3. **Nutrient Management**: Apply balanced NPK fertilizers (e.g., 250g N, 150g P2O5, 150g K2O per mature plant annually) complemented with well-rotted farmyard manure.
4. **Preventative Measures**: Foliar spray of copper oxychloride (0.3%) before monsoon rains provides effective prophylaxis against anthracnose and algal spots.
